Our Way · The full breakdown

You should know where your money goes.

Most travel platforms make this almost impossible to find out. We made it the first page of our brand. Here is exactly how Singular Places works — what we keep, what we don't, and why.

An open letter

A few uncomfortable numbers about how you travel.

When you book a €1,000 hotel stay on Booking.com, around €220 goes to a Dutch corporation. The hotel keeps the rest, but only after waiting weeks for payment, paying their own credit-card fees, and almost certainly compensating for the "best-price guarantee" by raising their rack rate elsewhere.

Of that €220, almost none of it returns to the place you visited. None of it funds the school in the village. None of it pays the keeper of the beehive next door. None of it preserves the road, the wetland, the language, the apprentice, the trade.

This isn't anyone's fault, exactly. It's just how the system was designed in the 1990s, and nobody has redesigned it since.

We started Singular Places because we were the hoteliers losing this money, and we were also the travellers paying it. We knew there was a different way to build it. Here it is, in full.

Our philosophy

Imperfect steps.

Most certifications for sustainable travel demand perfection at the outset. Twelve criteria, ten audits, a five-year roadmap, several thousand euros of consultant fees. Most small Hosts can't afford it, so they don't try — and the people doing the actual regenerative work get no recognition.

We disagree with that approach. Regeneration is a direction, not a destination. We measure every Host on 21 criteria from The Long Run's 4Cs framework — Conservation, Community, Culture, Commerce — and we publish the score. But we celebrate the next step, not the perfect one.

A Host moving from 60 to 70 in one year matters more to us than a Host already at 95. Because at 60, you're proving it can be done at all.

This is why our scores are public, this is why we keep updating them, and this is why we will not certify perfection. We are a community walking together, not a club checking IDs.

Questions worth asking

The objections we hear most often.

"Isn't this just greenwashing?"

Greenwashing is when a brand claims impact it cannot evidence. Our impact is auditable — the 14% commission is documented, the 10-10-80 split is contractual, the 10% of annual profit donated to global causes is published, and our partner The Long Run audits the framework annually. The Impact Report shows the numbers. If we ever start using vague claims, leave.

"Why is there no instant booking?"

Because a curated journey is not the same as a hotel reservation. When you message our Concierge, a human designs your stay — checks availability, calls the Host, suggests the Original that fits your dates. The wait is twenty-four hours; the trip is better. If instant booking is what you need, Booking.com exists, and we'd genuinely rather you used it for that.

"Won't this be more expensive?"

Same Host, same room, same date — we won't be cheaper, but we will rarely be meaningfully more expensive. You're not paying a markup for impact. You're choosing to spend the same money in a way that funds more of what the destination is asking the world to fund. The difference is where it ends up, not what it costs.

"Why only ~98 places?"

Because we have visited every one of them. Because the value of curation collapses at scale. Because Booking has 28 million listings and that is exactly why nobody trusts it for the trip that matters. Our target is 150 visible by end of 2026 — we grow as fast as we can grow without diluting why we exist, and not faster.
