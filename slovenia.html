The greenest country in Europe. 60% protected forest. More bee species per square kilometre than anywhere on the continent. A culture that still scythes hay by hand.

Why this country

The country that already does what the rest of Europe is trying to learn.

Slovenia is the only country in the world whose name contains the word love. It is also one of three European countries where you can drink directly from any mountain stream, and the first nation to declare bee-keeping a UNESCO intangible heritage.

We built our most active cluster here: 14 Hosts, 3 local Ambassadors, 2 NGOs we fund directly — one protecting Alpine wetlands, one teaching the next generation of hay-meadow farmers.

This page is not a brochure. It is the route we would send our own family on.

I Chapter one

Bohinj, not Bled.

Bled is the postcard. Bohinj is the place Slovenians go when they want to be left alone. Twenty minutes further into the Julian Alps, a single lake surrounded by national park, and a valley population of 4,800 — who still remember every cow by name.

You go here to walk the meadow that's been scythed by the same families for three centuries.

Two of our Hosts are here: Vila Planinka, the restored 1920s mountain home of Carla and Andrej, and Kmetija Pri Anžetu, a working dairy farm with three rooms above the cheese cellar.

4,800 people The population of the entire Bohinj Valley. Smaller than a single Berlin neighbourhood.
II Chapter two

The wine country no one visits.

On the eastern edge of Slovenia, where the country gently leans into Hungary, sits Štajerska — a wine region as old as Burgundy, with one-fiftieth of the tourism. The Romans planted vines here. The Habsburgs drank them. Nobody outside Central Europe has heard of any of it.

The bottles that never reach the export market — those are the ones worth flying for.

Four of our Hosts are wineries with rooms: Klet Brda (orange wine since 1957), Posestvo Pasji Rep (six rooms, six barrels), Domačija Mulec, and Hiša Linhart — a 17th-century manor restored by an Austrian couple who walked away from finance.

50× less tourism than Burgundy Same Roman vine heritage, almost identical latitude. Štajerska remains the wine region time forgot.
III Chapter three

The karst, and what lives below it.

Beneath southwestern Slovenia runs one of the most extensive cave systems in Europe — twenty-four kilometres of subterranean rivers and chambers, and the only known habitat of the Proteus anguinus, the "human fish" that lives a hundred years in darkness.

After the public closes the cave at six, twelve guests descend to a candlelit table inside Postojna.

Our Singular Original here — A Subterranean Dinner — happens twelve times a year, once a season, with a sommelier of Slovenian natural wines. Proceeds fund the speleobiology research station that protects the cave ecosystem. Arranged by Concierge only.

One species, 100 years The Proteus anguinus lives a century in pitch black. It exists only here.
